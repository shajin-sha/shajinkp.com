Winning two national hackathons solo, against teams of college students

By Shajin KP · Oct 28, 2026 · About 3 min read

First win
Conqueror National Level Hackathon, 1st prize. IHRD Tharang, Feb 2023
Second win
A national-level hackathon the year after, which made me a 2x champion at 18
Entered as
The only individual participant, both times

I've won two national-level hackathons. Both times I was the only person in the room competing alone. Everyone else came as a team.

The first one: Conqueror

My first win was the Conqueror National Level Hackathon, organised by IHRD Tharang in February 2023, where I took 1st prize. The project was built around machine learning and AI.

The second one, a year later

The next year I went back to another national-level hackathon and did the same thing again: no team, just me. There were around 30 to 40 hackers there. Every other participant was a college student.

It wasn't easy. I spent around two hours just planning the idea before writing anything, and at one point I honestly thought I was going to fail. My presentation was the worst part of the whole thing. I still managed to get through it, and I still won. That made me a 2x national hackathon champion at 18.

What actually decides a hackathon

After the second win I wrote down my "formula", half as a joke:

  • 50% the idea
  • 50% the final product
  • 0% presentation
  • 0% preparation
  • 0% luck

The zeros are exaggerated, but the point stands. Judges remember a sharp idea and a product that actually works. Being on a team, having polished slides, or having prepared for weeks matters much less than walking in with something real that does what you said it would.

If you're thinking of entering one

Don't wait until you have a team, and don't wait until you feel ready. Spend real time on the idea, then spend everything else making it work. Go and win.

Building something real and shipping it is also how DesignLumo, my first startup exit, came together.
